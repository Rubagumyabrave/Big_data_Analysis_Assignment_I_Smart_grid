# 5.5 Management recommendation

## Recommendation

**1. Operational actions**

- **Plan capacity for weekday middays and Monday restarts.** Network demand peaked on Monday 28 Sep at 13:00 (7,993.7 kW, 1.39× the average). Weekend demand is 21.3 % below weekdays, and weekly energy grew 6.2 % across September.
- **Target industrial customers first.** They are 10 % of meters but use 57.6 % of energy (17.5× residential per meter) and explain most regional differences (r = 0.97). Start demand-response and maintenance planning with Region 8.
- **Fix data completeness.** About 1.0 % of readings never arrive and 0.5 % arrive as duplicates. Monitor completeness per meter daily and investigate the worst meters (for example 1000003622, missing 47).
- **Publish daily reports at 03:00 CAT.** At midnight on 21 Sep the day's total was 0.10 % (152 kWh) short of the final figure, because late readings kept arriving until 02:45.
- **Use the median-ratio anomaly rule.** It detected 40 of 40 injected events with no false flags.

**2. Storage and reporting configuration**

- **Keep one-day chunks.** Per-meter aggregation took 1,275.6 ms with one-day chunks against 2,393.1 ms with 3-hour chunks, and one-day chunks were never meaningfully slower than 7-day chunks.
- **Compress chunks older than 48 hours, segmented by meter_id.** Storage falls from 1,356.7 MB to 501.6 MB (63 % saved), per-meter aggregation drops from 1,304.6 ms to 268.4 ms, and single-meter lookups stay fast (4.1 ms vs 3.4 ms). Time-only compression makes those lookups 28× slower (95.3 ms).
- **Build dashboards on the continuous aggregates.** The Aggregate dashboard refreshed in 493 ms against 12,891 ms for Raw (26.1× faster), with identical results. The cost is 17.93 MB of storage and a one-off 188 s refresh, with policies refreshing every 15 minutes.
- **Keep single-meter panels on the raw table.** The aggregate gave no gain there (0.7×), because the raw query is already an indexed lookup.

**3. Forecast model**

**Adopt the Ridge model (alpha = 0.1) for day-ahead regional forecasts.** On the 22–28 Sep test week (1,680 hourly predictions) its MAE was 4.557 kWh and RMSE 5.798 kWh, against 42.708 kWh and 79.403 kWh for the previous-day baseline: an 89 % reduction, or 0.77 % of an average hour. At each region's peak hour its mean error was 6.3 kWh against 159.9 kWh. The gain comes from learning the weekly pattern; the baseline misses by about 137 kWh per hour on Saturdays and Mondays.

Conditions for using it:

- Run it at 03:00 CAT, after late arrivals settle.
- Add a peak margin of about 3 % (the largest observed peak error was 2.2 %).
- Remember that targets omit about 1 % of readings.
- Retrain it weekly, because it was trained on one week of data.
- Fall back to the baseline if its MAE degrades.

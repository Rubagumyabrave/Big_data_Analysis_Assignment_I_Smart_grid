## Judgment

**Does the model improve on the baseline?** Yes, in every region and overall (MAE 4.557 vs 42.708 kWh).
The gain comes from the day-type switches: on Saturday (forecast from a weekday) and Monday (forecast
from a weekend day) the baseline misses by ~136-139 kWh per hour while the model stays at 3.6-4.9 kWh,
because dow and lag_168h tell it which kind of day is coming. On ordinary days both methods are
~4-5 kWh and the baseline is marginally better on three days, so the model's value is weekly
structure, not extra precision.

**Is it useful for utility planning?** Yes for day-ahead regional planning: a typical miss of
~4.6 kWh is under 1 % of an average hour, there is no meaningful bias (+0.4 kWh), and at each
region's peak hour the error is 1.3-16.7 kWh (at most 2.2 %), against up to 270 kWh (32 %) for the
baseline. 7 of 10 regional peaks fell on Monday 13:00, exactly the hour the baseline gets most wrong.

**Peak errors.** The largest model peak error is Region 6 (16.7 kWh, 2.2 %), so a planning margin of
a few percent above the forecast covers every observed peak. Errors are tested on one week only;
peaks driven by events the features do not capture (holidays, weather, outages) are not covered.

**Missing readings.** Targets are observed totals: 1,562-1,597 of 1,600 planned readings per
region-hour (~1 % missing). Actual demand is therefore ~1 % (~6 kWh per hour) above the targets,
which is the same size as the model's MAE. Further accuracy gains are not meaningful until
completeness improves; for supply planning the forecast could be scaled by planned/observed readings.

**Delayed arrivals in a live forecast.** This was an offline test on finalized totals. Live, at
00:00 the previous day is not yet complete: in 5.3, 531 readings (152.178 kWh, 0.10 %) of 21 Sep
arrived after midnight, from 21:15 onward, the last at 02:45. lag_24h for the late-evening hours
(and prev_day_mean) would be slightly too low, biasing those forecasts down. Running the forecast
at ~03:00 CAT, after late arrivals settle, or correcting lags by reading count, avoids this.

**Limitations.** Training uses one week (8-14 Sep), so each weekday is seen once; the data are
synthetic with a stable pattern; the model should be retrained regularly and monitored.

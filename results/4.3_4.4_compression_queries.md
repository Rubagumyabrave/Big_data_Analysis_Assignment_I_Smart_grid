## Explanation (4.4)

- Scans and aggregations (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5) are faster on both compressed tables: columnar storage
  reads only the needed columns, about a third of the bytes, and vectorised aggregation works on
  compressed batches.
- Q3 (group by meter) gains most with segmentby meter_id (268.4 ms): each compressed batch holds one
  meter, so grouping is cheap.
- Q4 (single meter) is the decisive difference. Time-only compression mixes all meters in each
  batch, so finding one meter means decompressing every batch in the range (95.3 ms, 28x slower).
  With segmentby meter_id the planner filters on the segment column before decompressing and
  reads only that meter's batches (4.1 ms, close to the 3.4 ms index lookup on the baseline).
- Recommendation: 1-day chunks with compression segmented by meter_id and ordered by event_time.
  It costs 63 MB more than time-only compression but keeps per-meter lookups fast and gives the
  best aggregate performance.
- Compression policies (jobs 1000, 1001, compress after 48 hours) were paused during the
  experiment and resumed afterwards (scheduled = t).
